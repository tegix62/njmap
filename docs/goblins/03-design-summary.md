# 03. The game as it stands (a draft for correction)

Written 2026-11-14 from the decisions log (01), in plain language. "(proposed)" marks anything the user has not accepted. If this page disagrees with the log, the log is the record and this page gets fixed. Read it as a check: is this the game you want?

## What it is

A strategy game inside the Townfinder app. Small clans (goblins, rats, gnomes, toads, a self-made clan, with rare dwarves and a lone rogue) fight over real New Jersey townships. It can be switched off, and the quiz, Drive and Progress modes stay as they are. Solo play is offline. A shared mode lets about four friends cooperate against AI clans, each leading a clan (duplicate clans are recoloured); any empty slot is held by an AI lord a friend can take over.

## How it feels

Opening it is as easy as the Today quiz: one tap, about two minutes, then stop or keep playing. The big detail sits below the surface and appears as you look for it. Chance is theatre: dice are shown rolling, force sizes are numbers, odds are words with reasons and never percentages, and a defeat always leaves you something.

## The map

Townships are the territory. Real rivers, ridges, highways and borders are the physics. What you have learned in the quiz sets your fog of war (known, familiar, rumoured, unknown) and an optional quiz answer can charge an action. You also learn by playing, and the game starts in a theatre around home and north-east Jersey. The real map is never changed, but small things it would not show can be invented (a beaver dam), and goblins can build their own bridge, slowly.

## Conflict at two speeds

- **Nameless garrisons: a quick skirmish.** Force numbers and dice, with losses on both sides. Concentrating force wins. You fortify your borders before you leave so enemies bleed against them.
- **A named captain defends: a front.** Contact, two or three maneuver beats that move a standing up or down a ladder in words (Behind, Even, Ahead, Commanding), a decision beat, and a closing tally. Maneuvering can win by itself, and a discovered weakness (a fear, a dam) is a shortcut, never a requirement. Direct force always works.
- You can have up to three fronts. A front is a short scene of about five beats that pauses when you leave. A lost battle frees you to focus elsewhere. Losing land is very rare.

## Characters

Three tiers in every clan (grunts, captains, legends). Named characters carry 3, 4 or 5 traits, of which 1, 2 or 3 are notable, plus injuries, fears tied to places, grudges, bonds, titles and scars. When a character's notable traits conflict under pressure there can be a rare closeup and a permanent choice. Death is possible, less often on your side, and a name can pass down a dynasty. Insults are player actions.

## Resources

Food, Build and Coin, with a town's real trade as flavour only. Goods move by real road on their own, as data and not simulated entities. Convoys are visible and can be raided. A convoy is where a small unwarned loss may happen, introduced by a scared little messenger and almost never taking a named character or your home.

## Time

The world ticks every hour with significant events around morning, noon and evening Eastern. No player can move that clock. Fronts run on your own time. In a sitting you can Advance your own affairs and spend the time on personal encounters. While you are away the world moves on elsewhere and you return to changes.

## The Director and fairness

A hidden Director paces the world in build, peak and relax, counting only active players. The dwarves are its hidden helping hand in timing, and a rule of the world in place: they come only from geologically fitting rock towns, either goaded by enemies or sought out by you. Nothing is taken from you without framing. A loss sits among other things that make it interesting.

## Rules for the writing

Real places, geology and industrial history; fictional characters. The Lenape are never a faction or a joke. The user writes the comedic content: names, traits, state words, any insult lines. The tools build the structure.
