# Paper test 01

Run 2026-11-14 in text, with Claude as the engine and the user as the lord of a goblin clan. Purpose: feel the core loop (D76 to D78), the resolution style (D102) and the first force and character rules. All names and trait words are placeholders (the user writes the real ones, D68). The dice were real random rolls.

## Setting

Goblins hold Paterson. Rats hold Totowa under Rat Captain R. Clifton and Woodland Park are nearby. Geography was first written from memory and unchecked. During the test it was "corrected" against the app's data to say there is no river between Paterson and Totowa. That correction was itself unreliable: a second check claimed the Passaic ran along about half of the Paterson–Totowa border, and a third, evenly sampled check (2026-11-15) shows it does not run along it at all, so Paterson–Totowa is a land border (H42). What did hold: Totowa lies 4.0 miles west-southwest of Paterson, they share a border, and Clifton and Totowa do not touch.

## Hidden state (revealed at the end)

- **Rat Captain R**, tier 2: notable traits Proud (never retreats while his rats watch) and Cautious (keeps a rear guard); minor traits Hoarder and Light sleeper; fear: fire; confidence high. The player never learned the minor traits.
- **Forces:** goblins 18 grunts plus captains Moss (Sharp-eyed, Patient; Loud, Sleeps in) and Flint (Reckless, Stubborn; Hungry, Forgetful). Rats: R and 8 at the border, 4 rear guard, 4 on a convoy.
- **Engine:** 2d6 plus modifiers (+2 a known trait fits, +1 a discovered fact, -2 a known strength, +2 exploiting a discovered fear, plus hidden modifiers). Bands: 10 and over clean win; 8 to 9 win with a cost; 5 to 7 setback with a gain; 4 or less disaster with a lesson.

## Log

| Beat | Choice | Roll | Band | What happened |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Scout the rat camp (Moss) | 3+3=6, +2 = 8 | Win with a cost | Learned R is Proud. The rats were alerted (Moss is Loud) |
| 2 | Taunt R from the border | 1+6=7, +2 = 9 | Win with a cost | R charged out; a foothold on the border; learned R is Cautious; R holds a grudge against Moss |
| 3 | Hold the border (Flint and 9 against R and 8) | 1+1=2, +3 = 5 | Setback with a gain | 4 goblins fell and Flint was scarred; R lost 3 rats. A closeup (Proud against Cautious) rolled 2+3=5: R broke, retreated, and is Humiliated, with a new fear of the border |
| 4 | Work on the border fear by night (Moss) | 1+2=3, +3 = 6 | Setback with a gain | A goblin was caught and Moss grazed; the rats doubled the watch; discovered R fears fire |
| 5 | The player ended the conflict and rested | none | none | The user was mentally checked out and called it a loss |

The user passed on intercepting the convoy in beat 3 ("too big a loss to risk"); it reached Totowa.

**Tally:** goblins lost 5 grunts and two captains were marked; rats lost 3. The convoy delivered its Food. The foothold held. The player gained four discoveries (Proud, Cautious, the border fear, fire).

## What the test found

1. **It worked as a balance of chance and choice.** After a snake-eyes roll that still ended well, the user said it "felt like a very reasonable outcome, a steady balance of chance and choice" (supports H31).
2. **Scouting did not dominate.** It led to levers, which led to gambits, and a repeated gambit was harder because the information goes stale (H41).
3. **A conflict with no end feels flat.** Four beats of mostly middling outcomes ended in a stalemate. The user checked out, ended it, and called it a loss. The tally agrees with them on body count, even though they gained four discoveries that the game never counted as progress (H44).
4. **Too much observing of cause and effect.** Each beat listed modifiers as plus and minus numbers, which turned every result into reading maths. The reasons should be a few words or icons (H45).
5. **The bands were damped.** The dice rolled low (6, 7, 2, 3), and the modifiers pulled every result into the middle bands. Nothing was decisive (H44).
6. **The text medium inflates observation.** Long narration in text is slower than a map and a short animation. Some of the felt slowness is an artifact of the test. The pacing problem is still real.
7. **The player wants a way to disengage** and a clear verdict at the end (H44).
8. **Missing at the start:** a map, force sizes, and a garrison rule (H42, H43).
