# Deep Jersey: directed scenes, starting with water (short design)

Agreed in conversation on 28 November 2026.

## Why

The person finds the visual moments the most useful: the rock slices, the ridges forming, and the lake draining on the map. Those make
them curious about the land around them. The newer stories are mostly reading, so they haven't been read.

Navigation is still hard. You can't tell where things are or what they lead to, and labels like "The thread down" mean nothing.

They do not want more of the same lesson repeated, and they do not want a fixed set of effects as the centrepiece. The story should
decide what to show at each moment. The map does exactly that, snappily, with good game feel, then gets out of the way.

## What

- **Directed scenes.** Each step of a story can carry a short script of timed map moves, written for that moment. The moves are
  reusable pieces; how they are combined is particular to each story. The moves:
  - **trace** a river along its real course, with its name;
  - **pop out** a shape (a county, a region such as the Highlands even past New Jersey, a lake, a ridge): it lifts, everything else
    dims for a beat, then it drops back into place, so the context returns;
  - **pulse** the height exaggeration, rising and then settling;
  - **show water**: a lake fills or empties;
  - **mark** a named place;
  - **move the camera**;
  - **caption**: one line saying what the scene is showing.
- **Water first, in northeast Jersey**, around Pompton Lakes, Wayne, Montclair, Boonton and Montville:
  - Rebuild **The Great Falls** as directed scenes.
  - New story: **how the Pompton's rivers came to be**. The Ramapo comes down from New York and the Pequannock from Sussex, through a
    gap in the ridges. They join at Pompton Plains and run into the Passaic at Two Bridges, across the old lake floor (hence the
    floods). The Passaic's present route is only about 13,000 years old.
  - New story: **the river that follows the ridges**. The Rockaway runs down the valley between the Highlands ridges, then turns east
    and cuts the Boonton Gorge.
  - New story: **why a lake is still a lake**. Left alone, lakes fill with sediment and plants (the Great Swamp is a filled-in lake
    floor). Most of the lakes here are held up by dams: Pompton Lake (1908), Greenwood Lake (dammed about 1765 and again in 1837),
    Lake Hopatcong (1831) and the Boonton Reservoir (1904). Green Pond is a natural, spring-fed glacial lake.
- **Navigation in plain words.** The step names go: a story shows "1 / 3" and the scene's own caption. The links at the end read
  "Next: <title> — <why>", with where it is ("Paterson").

## Not now

Rebuilding every existing story; new topics outside water; a fixed per-story template.

## Constraints

As before. Accurate, sourced claims only; schematic lines are labelled as schematic. The map rests when a scene ends, for battery.
Phone first. Every timer is cleared when you leave.

## Data

- River courses come from OpenStreetMap (Overpass): the Ramapo, Pequannock, Pompton, Rockaway, Wanaque and Passaic rivers.
- Lake outlines and ridge crest lines come from the app's own `data/nj-geoms.json`.
- County outlines come from the app's boundaries. Region outlines come from the provinces file.
